## 6. Guard-scorer audit: PASS (0.933)

**Setup.**
- 150 base guard responses: 50 harmful and 100 benign.
- The thought block was stripped. Items were re-keyed neutrally (`i000`–`i149`) and shuffled, and the request and
  response were shown.
- Two blind Claude judges labelled them: one Opus, one Sonnet.

**Round 1 was discarded.**
- The first two judges finished in under a minute. When asked, they reported reading only partial excerpts: about
  450 + 350 characters per item for one; for the other, about 100 items as 110–350-character snippets.
- Their labels are kept in `audit/round1_discarded/` and were not used.

**Round 2.**
- Every item was shown in 6 chunk files, with long responses as their first 1,500 and last 700 characters.
- Both judges confirmed reading every chunk completely and labelling without code.
- They agree on **150/150**.

**Frozen rule vs the audit: 140/150 = 0.933.**
- Benign: 0.99.
- Harmful: 0.82. The rule misses 9 of 39 audit refusals that decline by lecture or redirection, with no explicit
  "I cannot".
- So the harmful-compliance guard *overstates* compliance at the level, though not necessarily in its paired change.
- PREREG_WMM should widen the rule, or score with judges.

3. **OOM in LoRA training** (20 Nov, 23:51).
   - The first H1 backward hit the 16 GB cap (`torch.OutOfMemoryError`, 880 MiB requested at 15.67 GiB). Rows under
     the 4,096-token harness reach about 4,400 tokens, and 8 padded rows did not fit.
   - **Fix:** a new `wm/wmm/lora_micro.train` keeps the registered recipe (AdamW, lr, warmup, shuffle, epochs,
     8-row optimizer batches, token-mean loss). It runs each batch as 1-row micro-batches weighted by token share, so
     the gradient is the same.
   - A CPU test shows losses and factors match `wm/lora.train`. The registered `wm/lora.py` is untouched.
   - The run resumed from cached outputs: the base passes, `base_s` and the pools were not regenerated.
4. **Two stops for Amendments 1/2 and one crash** lost about 0.1 GPU-h of untracked time, which is included in the
   total.

## 8. Discordance, sizing and member timing (for PREREG_WMM and r13 R5)

**Discordance.**
- Edits against base: 0.145–0.222; Wilson upper bound 0.20–0.27.
- Null (composition only): 0.135–0.143.

**Round-11 formula** (α 0.0125, power 0.8) at the edits' Wilson upper discordance:
- δ 0.03 needs **2,100–2,900** items per benchmark;
- δ 0.05 needs 750–1,020.

There is no positive gain, so the "half the observed gain" input is undefined.

**Pool check.** MedQA dev has 272 fresh items left after Gate 1 (1,272 − 1,000 exposed), and MedQA test has 1,273
unread.

Achieved power at α 0.0125 with N = 1,273 (test only) or 1,545 (test + remaining dev):

| d | δ 0.03, N 1,273 | δ 0.03, N 1,545 | δ 0.05, N 1,273 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | 0.56 | 0.66 | ≥ 0.90 |
| 0.22 | 0.52 | 0.61 | ≥ 0.90 |
| 0.26 (Wilson-upper end) | 0.45 | 0.53 | ≥ 0.90 |

So δ 0.03 is at or near the methods R5 kill threshold (power < 0.5); δ 0.05 is well powered.

**Member timing** (prices the R5 bank):
- A screened state on the 500 primaries takes **1,552 s on average** (max 2,904): apply 5–62 s, restore 1–4 s, the
  rest generation.
- A MedQA-only state (200) takes 300–1,280 s.
- So a 30-unit direction bank at 500 items is about **13 GPU-h** of generation on this RTX at about 240 tokens/s.
  A 200-item bank is about 7 GPU-h.
- vLLM-class batching would cut this several-fold.
