**E9 — L98–99, G3/G4: the part a reviewer would call a moving goalpost.**
 1. *Reuse of seen outcomes.* The WikiText D3 θ̂ (0.0071) and all six intervals are already known; PREREG A4.6 discloses this for H1w. Part B reruns "registered H1w folds, nuisance and six intervals" with new features on the same outcomes. That is development on an examined panel. Add: "Part B is exploratory development evidence. Field and chart code, the arm list and the thresholds are frozen by hash in amendment A5 before the first field is computed; it cannot confirm anything." Remove "pass" and "Kill" for Part B, or rename them "go / no-go for buying sealed rows".
 2. *Pass/kill language.* PREREG A4.4 forbids "pass/kill label and no 'kill' language" for H1w, and §4 allows a kill only on "a qualified adequately powered panel". Part B has lower bound power 0.60 on 229 rows and lower on 167 (representation r2 §3, "re-simulate before reading outcomes" — omitted from L99). Add the power caveat to L99 and make the stopping rule "upper 95 % < 0.05 **and** simulated power ≥ 0.80 at 0.15", otherwise "unresolved".
 3. *Estimand change.* PREREG θ is 1 − A/B for StatNN against isotonic norm. L99 uses "θ(StatNN+field or chart) − θ(StatNN) ≥ 0.15", a difference of two θ values, which is a different quantity from world-model's "≥ 15 % residual-MAE reduction" and from PREREG §7's 5 % screen for H1b. Define one: "relative residual-MAE reduction of arm X over augmented StatNN, row-summed, same bootstrap-t". Say which of the 0.15 and 0.05 is the go line and why 0.15 is not a threshold chosen after seeing the 0.129 MMLU θ̂.
 4. *Multiplicity.* "StatNN+field **or** chart" lets the better of two arms pass; with nine arms and three controls there is a forking-paths problem. Name one primary arm (the chair's choice) and treat the rest as descriptive, or apply Holm.
 5. *Part A.* A one-sided lower 95 % binomial bound > 0.5 needs ≥ 12 of 15 sign agreements (≥ 80 %), so with a likely 15–30 resolved pairs the gate is strict and the chance of "inconclusive" is high; say so. The ladder criterion (≥ 0.7 at s = 0.25) is a point threshold with no resolved-pair rule or interval, at n ≈ 40 where the standard error is ≈ 0.07; give it the same rule or call it descriptive. L98 drops the + q(D) − q(R) term of representation's prediction; state which sign predictor is registered.

**E11 — L118–119, paper policy.** "A positive result becomes a second paper, a negative one a section of the first" makes the venue depend on the sign of the result. A TMLR reviewer will read that as outcome-contingent reporting, and TMLR's criterion (claims supported by evidence, not novelty) gives no reason to split. It is also unsafe on the positive side: a Part A/B pass is exploratory development evidence on examined data, not a result. Replace with:
> "Paper 1 (measurement and negative-result paper) reports the follow-up programme as designed and as run, whatever happens: Part A and Part B appear as an exploratory section with the A5 disclosure, and a failed Part A is reported with its intervals. A second paper is justified only if the sealed confirmatory purchase (G7) is run and passes its own registration; a pass on D3 alone is reported in paper 1 as a screen. Paper 1 is not edited until G3/G4 report, but its limitations paragraph already discloses that the follow-up was designed after seeing the H1w and MMLU results."
This is defensible for TMLR if the split is by evidence level (exploratory vs confirmed), not by sign.
