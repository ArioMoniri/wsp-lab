## 1. Is the registered primary dead? WikiText as primary?

### 1a. MMLU-Pro gold log-prob: dead as a confirmatory H1a endpoint

**Decision: yes. H1a is *untested*, not falsified, and the endpoint cannot be rescued by changing the rule.**

- A2.1 says in terms that failure of (i), (ii) or (iii) "leaves the endpoint unqualified (H1a untested)". (ii) fails (max h = 0.1137 > 0.10) and (iii) fails (P upper 97.5% = 0.2264 > 0.20; point 0.176). (i), the repeat bounds and reliability pass. A2.1 also listed, as the evidence that reverses it to the strict reading, "P (upper 97.5%) > 0.20" and "any h_i > δ"; both occurred.
- A2.1's clause "exceedances that are not confined to large-effect LoRAs (a scorer problem)" needs reading, not waiving. In letter the exceedances are not confined to LoRAs: 21/60 lora_sft, 20/139 chain, 1/30 task_vector. In substance they are confined to large-effect rows: h_i tracks |y_i| with Spearman 0.92; the 42 rows have median |y| 0.513 against 0.008 for the other 187; median h/|y| over the 42 rows is 0.13, range 0.06–0.62 (largest-effect chains: h/|y| 0.068 at y ≈ −1.67). Repeat discrepancy is exactly 0. So this is not a scorer defect. It is the same finding as A1.2 for KL: an **absolute** δ/2 precision limit is the wrong shape for an endpoint whose item half-width scales with the effect.
- I rejected widening δ and "scope by |y|" on 5 Nov as outcome-fitted, and I reject them again. The same applies to loosening the 0.20 tripwire or the δ ceiling now: the overshoots are 14% (ceiling) and 13% (tripwire upper bound), small enough that the temptation is exactly the one the registration exists to block. A relative criterion (for example h_i ≤ max(δ/2, c|y_i|)) is the right *design* for the next endpoint version, but adopted now it is a new endpoint registered after seeing this failure, on the same development panel that exposed it. It may be proposed for a future study, never applied to this one.
- Consequence: the 106-component sealed MMLU panel cannot yield a confirmatory H1a claim under any outcome. Note the independent reason from §B: even if MMLU had qualified, the power gate fails (§3).

### 1b. WikiText log-PPL as the primary: **not as H1a; yes as a named exploratory claim**

WikiText log-PPL is qualified on every A2.1 condition with wide margins (max h 0.0128 against δ = 0.0488; panel-mean upper 0.0049 against 0.0244; no entry over δ/2; reliability lower bound 0.9923; repeat 0). Measurement is not the obstacle. I nevertheless do not allow it to *replace* the primary of H1a, for four reasons.

1. **It is a different scientific claim.** H1a (approved wording) is readability of the predecessor-relative *task-likelihood* change. WikiText log-PPL change is generic distributional damage. Registration §6 already says KL and WikiText "cannot pass H1a", and SYNTHESIS §2 row 1 accepted the KL analogue only as "secondary and bounded… KL-only success does not pass H1a, justify capability language, or release money". Swapping the endpoint after the primary failed qualification would convert that rule into its opposite.
2. **Forking paths.** The switch would be chosen *because* MMLU failed and WikiText passed on the development panel, with the MMLU D3 θ̂ (0.129) known. The disclosure can be honest, but the label "primary" would still lend it confirmatory weight it has not earned.
3. **No confirmatory upside.** The §5 gate has not been run for WikiText, but the binding constraints are shared: the same 106 sealed components, n_eff ≈ 40 on D3, and loss mass concentrated in a few large rows. The MMLU gate gives lower power bounds of 0.43–0.47 at θ = 0.15 against the 0.80 required. I expect WikiText to land in the same range unless its residuals are markedly better behaved; that is testable on CPU (below). A primary that will still be labelled exploratory is a secondary.
4. **Sealed WikiText windows do not exist.** A2.5(4) kept the 64 development windows as the descriptive endpoint for fresh rows too. A fresh-window confirmatory WikiText claim would need a new sealed window panel (distinct hash domain, disjoint from the 64 development windows) committed before scoring.

**Is "weight structure predicts the perplexity change beyond family + norm" a meaningful H1a?** It is a meaningful but **weaker** claim, and it should carry another name. It says that, after removing family and log incremental norm, StatNN on weight statistics beats an isotonic function of raw norm at predicting generic LM damage. That is a *sensitivity* statement (which structure of ΔW governs how much the model's overall distribution moves), the same class as the KL result the council bounded. It is useful for edit-safety and damage forecasting. It does not say anything about whether *task behaviour* is readable from weights. Perplexity damage is also the endpoint most likely to be explained by norm plus location, so the mandatory flexible family×spline(norm) nuisance sensitivity (§3 of the registration) carries more weight here than for MMLU: if it erases the gain the structural reading is withdrawn.

### 1c. Interval procedure given that none passed

- **Declare no confirmatory interval possible; report all, none decisive.** That is what A2.3's fixed fallback order already says ("neither passes: H1a is exploratory; estimates and intervals only").
- **I reject "BCa-conditional as least-bad".** On the report's own table BCa is the *worst* calibrated: coverage 0.9025–0.9073 against ≥ 0.93, null rejection 0.043–0.048 against ≤ 0.035. Bootstrap-t is closer (coverage 0.919–0.925; null rejection 0.035–0.037 on the empirical scenario, 0.036–0.037 under pair-merging, failing mainly on joint null size and coverage).

- **Robustness of the D3 conclusion to the choice.** Lower bounds on D3: −0.003 (conditional bootstrap-t), −0.002 (joint), −0.008 and −0.005 (BCa); my own conditional percentile replicate +0.002 [0.002, 0.239]. The conclusion does not depend on the interval; that is the sentence the paper can use.

## 2. Interpretation of θ̂ = 0.129 versus 0.007

**Short answer: the leave-out result does not show that the residual signal is an artefact of item variance. It shows that whatever signal exists lives in the large-effect rows, and it cannot be told apart from zero in either direction.**

### Evidence (CPU re-analysis, descriptive; D3 stratum, 229 rows, 120 components)

### Interpretation

1. **Item variance cannot manufacture a positive θ.** Noise in y enters |r − p| for both predictors and attenuates the ratio toward zero; the item-split and the near-equal conditional and joint intervals confirm that item sampling is not the driver.
2. **The leave-out is confounded with effect size.** h is almost a function of |y|. Dropping h > δ/2 drops the rows where the residuals are large relative to the item noise (median h/|r| 0.21 on those rows against 0.11 on the rest, so the noise is not worse where the signal is). The 187 remaining rows have median |y| = 0.008, below δ/10; no predictor can show structure beyond norm at an effect that the endpoint is not designed to resolve.
3. **But the difference 0.129 vs 0.007 is not itself resolved.** The leave-out interval [−0.20, 0.15] contains 0.129, and the full-panel interval contains 0. The statistically honest reading of D3 is θ ∈ ≈ [0, 0.24], with the point estimate carried by roughly a fifth of the rows. The subgroup θ = 0.266 is selected on a variable (h) that is a function of the outcome and is therefore biased in an unknown direction (compare "drop largest B", −0.139). Treat it as description, not as an effect size.
4. **What is real so far:** (i) the labels are reproducible and reliable (repeat 0, reliability 0.994); (ii) the measured advantage over isotonic-on-norm replicates across item halves; (iii) it is concentrated in LoRA-SFT and in high-norm edits. **What is not established:** that it is nonzero at the 95% level on any registered interval; that it is "beyond norm" under a flexible family×norm nuisance (not yet reported); or that it would hold on fresh components.

### What settles it

- **Not** loss weighting by 1/h_i². Because h ∝ |y|, it would down-weight exactly the rows that carry the signal and concentrate the estimand on rows at or below δ. It is an outcome-derived weight, changes the estimand and would have lower power. **Not** restricting to h_i ≤ δ/2 "by design": that is selection on the outcome, which A2.2 forbids. A descriptive weighted analysis is acceptable as a sensitivity (never as a decision).
- **Yes:** a prospective, outcome-free stratification. For any future estimate, define the strata by family and incremental-norm tertile (weight-only), pre-specify the stratum θ, and use the high-norm/LoRA stratum as the pre-declared "readability beyond norm in material edits" claim, with the sensitivity that includes the low-norm stratum. On D3 the strata are already above (0.195 and 0.230), but they were looked at after the fact and cannot be counted.
- **Yes, CPU-only and cheap, before any further compute:** report the registered flexible family×spline(norm) nuisance and the equal-component-weight ratio (both mandatory in §3 and not yet in the report), the family×norm-tertile table above, and the item-split replication, on MMLU and on WikiText (A4).
- **The decisive test** remains fresh components, and it cannot be decisive with the sealed panel (§3).

## 3. Decision: (c) stop here for GPU scoring

**Decision: (c).** Do not score the sealed panel. Keep the partition sealed (`ebf0379a15d9…`) and unspent. Report the measurement/benchmark paper with the D3 exploratory predictor result as the final predictor evidence. Spend CPU only: the §2 companions, plus the A4 WikiText gate if the owner wants H1w.

### Reason

1. **The sealed run cannot answer the question it was built for.** From the gate table, empirical scenario: P(reject) at θ = 0.15 is 0.45–0.49 (lower power bound 0.43–0.47), 0.66–0.70 at 0.20 and 0.82–0.86 at 0.25; P(decision pass) at 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.25 is only 0.055–0.072 / 0.19–0.23 / 0.42–0.47 (empirical scenario); the mean half-width at θ = 0.15 is 0.155–0.169, and at θ = 0 it is 0.18–0.20, so an upper bound below 0.05 (the kill rule) is out of reach. Crudely scaling half-width with 1/√n, rejection power 0.80 at 0.15 needs ≈ 2.5× the sealed 106 components and the §4 precision condition (half-width ≤ θ̂/2) ≈ 5×; this is my arithmetic from the table, not a simulation. The run would add one more estimate of the same kind as D3, with a probability of roughly one half of crossing zero again if θ is near 0.15.
2. **The endpoint is unqualified**, so the run could not even carry a registered "confirmatory" or "kill" label; at best it is an exploratory estimate with an item-population claim withdrawn.
3. **My own 5 Nov stopping criterion is met.** I wrote that the owner should *not* buy the fresh scoring unless knowingly if "the D3-OOF gate gives a conditional lower power bound below 0.50 for every interval procedure". It is 0.454, 0.474, 0.464 (conditional) and 0.448, 0.471, 0.434 (joint). I stay consistent.
4. **The sealed panel is a one-shot asset.** Registration §2 says exposed components cannot be re-used for confirmation. The panel is already ≈ 2.5–5× too small for a future confirmatory design, but it is the only fresh stratum material the zoo has; spending it on an exploratory estimate removes the option for nothing the paper needs.
5. **Budget is not the reason.** The cost is affordable: ≈ 4.5 GPU-h, plus the A3 bitwise re-check (≈ 0.2 h) and an unbuilt sealed item panel (CPU/engineering), against ≈ 21 h of the 30 h core still unspent after ≈ 9 h on TF-v1. The user's defaults (TF-v1 yes, 30 h yes, a measurement/benchmark paper accepted as success, no extra history, no new Lambda) all permit stopping here; none of them requires spending the remainder. I do not recommend spending it merely because it is available.

### Why not (a) or (b)

- **(a) sealed MMLU as a registered exploratory study (4.5 h):** acceptable only if the owner buys it knowingly for an estimate with the properties in point 1. I do not recommend it. If the owner chooses it, minimum conditions: A3 bitwise check first; the sealed item panel committed first; a dated amendment stating "exploratory, no pass/kill labels, P_fresh audit reported, estimates and all six intervals with the all-six wording rule"; the sealed panel scored once; no row, interval or endpoint chosen after unsealing.
- **(b) WikiText fresh-only as primary:** not as primary (§1b). Cost, from `stage_seconds` in the eval records (model load ≈ 17 s, WikiText stage ≈ 4.3 s, other stages not needed): ≈ 0.4 min per delta × 281 deltas ≈ 1.9 GPU-h (derived, not measured), plus a sealed window panel. Only worth running if the CPU gate in A4.5 passes. Not before.

### Evidence that would reverse the decision

- **To (b):** the A4.5 CPU gate on WikiText D3 OOF losses meets (a)–(e) at the registered scale (lower power bound ≥ 0.80 at θ = 0.15 for conditional and joint, null size ≤ 0.035, coverage ≥ 0.93, denominator failures < 0.1%), *and* the flexible family×spline(norm) nuisance leaves θ̂ above 0. Then H1w can become confirmatory on sealed windows (≈ 1.9 h).
- **To (a):** (i) an owner decision that the exploratory fresh replication is worth ≈ 5 h; or (ii) CPU companions showing a positive lower bound on at least the percentile interval *and* a flexible-nuisance θ̂ ≥ 0.10, which would make a fresh replication more likely to be informative (though it would still be exploratory).
- **To a larger programme (outside this cap and outside the user's defaults):** funding for ≳ 270–500 stratum components with a relative-precision endpoint registered first. Not recommended inside the 19 November window.
- **Toward stopping everything including H1w:** WikiText flexible-nuisance θ̂ ≤ 0, or a power lower bound < 0.50 again. Then the paper reports the endpoint qualification and the MMLU result only.

H1b (§7) is unaffected: its development-only screen proceeds on D3 under its own gates, with no H1a unsealing.
