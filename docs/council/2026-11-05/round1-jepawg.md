# Council round 1: JEPA and weight-space dynamics

## What was actually learned

[Bifurcations from Weight Space](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30847) builds a parameter-indexed zoo, **not sequences of training checkpoints**. At each physical control parameter p, several simulated state trajectories train a common p-conditioned causal convolutional forecaster, then a copy is fine-tuned for 30 epochs at that p. JEPAWG trains on paired (p, final weights θ); it predicts the weight embedding from p, then generates forecaster weights. Those weights are used to forecast physical states; known bifurcation locations are validation ground truth, not labels used to fit JEPAWG. The paper's Table 6 reports Lorenz 800 p values × 5 initial conditions, Δt 0.02, training length 20 time units; Rössler 1,000 × 10, Δt 0.1, length 20; Brusselator 1,024 × 5, Δt 0.05, length 30; Hénon 1,024 × 5, 800 iterations. These lengths are physical simulation lengths, not optimizer steps. It also reports special interpretability grids, including 1,540 Hénon cascade points. The continuous solver step is 0.001. Each JEPA and decoder stage runs 5,000 optimization steps, five seeds. The paper gives 7,857 as an example weight-vector size in its baseline architecture.

The exact stage-one objective in §3.1 is 25‖P(Ep(p)) − Eθ(θ)‖² + 25[V(Eθ(θ)) + V(Ep(p))], where V(z) = mean_j max(0, 1 − std_batch(z_j)); covariance weight is zero. It freezes both encoders and P, then trains D by weight-space MSE ‖D(P(Ep(p))) − θ‖². Its response G(p) = ‖∇p D(P(Ep(p)))‖F measures sensitivity of **generated network weights to the physical control parameter**, not a time derivative or score gradient; peaks are compared with independently known bifurcations. The decoder smooths per-run fitting noise, but its response is limited by sampling resolution and what the observed trajectories contain.

[Weight-Space Physics](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07127) instead maps two lattice couplings to weights of a flow sampler; its weight encoder sees 19,689 neural ODE weights. A shared conditional flow is trained for 30,000 steps, then each coupling is fine-tuned for at most 1,000 steps. Its covariance-free VICReg and separately trained decoder have the same conceptual structure; the full covariance variant is an ablation. These are learned correspondences over structured physical parameter grids, not a general guarantee for arbitrary weight edits.

Our situation differs at every causal link: post-hoc heterogeneous edits of a 0.8B model, short chains of 2–6 edits, and benchmark rates rather than known physical control parameters ([brief](BRIEF.md); [suite review](../../reviews/P4/board-a-d3-suite-2026-11-04.md)). Sampled rates have substantial seed noise, while MMLU-Pro and HellaSwag are repeatable at this tier; do not conflate those target types. No scalar physical parameter or known smooth bifurcation chart is supplied. The papers' common initialization partially fixes weight symmetries; our common base helps, but different edit procedures need not trace a common smooth chart. Smooth decoded weights alone would not establish smooth capability or causal transitions.

[W2T](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15990) resolves LoRA factorization ambiguity with QR then SVD and uses rank- and position-level transformers; this concerns LoRA updates, not arbitrary dense edits. I fetched its [repository](https://github.com/xiaolonghan2000/Weight2Token), README, `llm/common/w2t_models.py`, and `llm/regression/perf_prediction_pipeline.py`. Its implementation and paper support this narrower comparison, not a claim that our pooled spectra preserve behaviorally decisive directions. The JEPAWG papers' HTML did not identify a corresponding public implementation; the linked Ranger optimizer is not JEPAWG code. I cannot verify JEPAWG implementation details beyond the papers.

## Implications for our inductive biases

M2 assumes an ordered hidden state can exploit history; M5 gives the sequence a flexible attention-based interaction model. Neither has evidence of a stable temporal law for these heterogeneous edits, and 2–6 positions provide little leverage. M3's cumulative-delta JEPA assumes adjacent feature states admit a predictable latent transition and that its auxiliary objective preserves score-relevant variation. That is a hypothesis, not JEPAWG's parameter-to-weight pairing. `src/wsp/predictor/models/`, `data.py`, `train.py`, and `splits.py` implement the actual comparison; see also [architectures](../../ARCHITECTURES.md) and [candidates](../../MODEL_CANDIDATES.md). Keep M3 as an inexpensive controlled research ablation, not the default or as evidence of JEPAWG replication.

StatNN's pooled statistics and trees impose useful low-sample bias: thresholds and nonlinear interactions need fewer fitted degrees of freedom than a learned sequence representation, and family/scale signals can already explain substantial variation. This is a plausible explanation, not a demonstrated causal attribution. The [brief](BRIEF.md) reports trajectory-split MMLU/HellaSwag ρ 0.57/0.65 for StatNN versus 0.35/0.32 M2 and 0.15/0.11 M3; held-out-family results remain poor. A tree can exploit family confounding, so within-family and ancestry-cluster evaluation matter.

A planning estimate, not a measured crossover: screen at 1,000–3,000 **independent ancestry clusters** with diverse, deliberately varied edit orders and repeated families, then extend only if a learning curve projects a crossover. Reason: six families already imply roughly 170–500 independent examples/family at that scale, versus at most ~50 rows/family at 300 before ancestry dependence and held-out folds. Short correlated chains do not multiply independent sample size, and no amount of data guarantees a win if history contains no conditional information beyond cumulative features. Measure paired history-versus-stateless curves at matched cluster counts and capacity, with cluster bootstrap, not a fixed universal threshold.

## Ranked experiments (costs are marginal estimates)

Use protocol v2, training-only transforms, ancestry-cluster splits/bootstrap, per-family centered metrics and admissible deterministic targets. Pretraining must exclude all held-out ancestry clusters, including their unlabeled descendants; a separate explicitly transductive arm may use the full 1,235 but cannot be compared as inductive generalization. Timings from [brief](BRIEF.md): 66 s/extraction, 1–10 min/predictor run, 7.5 min/eval entry here or 3.1 min effective H100 with three workers. GPU-hour estimates exclude engineering and assume cached features unless stated; multi-seed and folds multiply runs.

1. **Geometry and conditional history audit.** In train folds standardize features, compare within-family nearest-neighbor distances and local PCA residuals against family- and norm-matched shuffled controls, and test whether parameter distance predicts feature distance on held-out ancestry clusters. Compare order-shuffled and history-ablated M2 with matched stateless controls. Prediction: if a smooth family manifold exists, held-out local reconstruction and distance ordering improve over matched permutations; if history matters, true order beats shuffle with positive paired cluster interval. Cost: CPU geometry plus 6 predictor runs ≈ 0.1–1 GPU-hour; add runs for all protocol folds/seeds. Add a diagnostic module and order-shuffle option in `data.py`, report through `train.py`; retain `splits.py` ancestry boundaries. Failure means do not spend on sequence scaling.
2. **Train-only unlabeled pretraining.** Add a compact encoder trained on masked pooled features or genuine predecessor→successor pairs with VICReg variance guard, then freeze and fit a small supervised head; compare identical architecture from scratch, PCA and StatNN, same splits and tuning budget. Prediction: at least +0.05 paired ρ on both deterministic targets with intervals excluding zero across seeds; otherwise discard. Check variance and effective rank to detect collapse. Start with 6 pretrain/fine-tune runs, ≈ 0.1–1 GPU-hour by existing predictor timings, subject to measurement; 1,235 uncached extractions would cost ≈22.6 GPU-hours, but use cached features where available. Add pretraining entry point and fold-scoped cache in `train.py`/`data.py`, encoder and masked-view objective in `models/`.
3. **Latent delta and composition consistency.** Encode cumulative states z_t and predict z_{t+1} − z_t from z_t and edit features; for chains with actual intermediate states compare composed two-step prediction to the observed final latent, with stop-gradient target and variance regularization. Fit score head only after or alongside pretraining. A consistency loss cannot invent missing intermediate data or assume edits commute. Prediction: lower held-out latent transition error than a zero-delta and linear baseline, and +0.05 paired score ρ over matched stateless head; otherwise stop. Six runs ≈ 0.1–1 GPU-hour, subject to measured training runtime; no new evaluation if labels/features exist. Add transition-pair batching/masks in `data.py`, model/loss in `models/`, and nested loss-weight selection in `train.py`.
4. **Buy independent coverage only after signal audit.** If learning curves and history ablations support it, evaluate 700 new independently sourced, balanced edits to reach ~1,000 entries, with purposeful order reversals and ancestry recorded. Prediction: matched history model improves ≥0.05 ρ over stateless on both benchmarks with positive paired cluster interval, increasing with cluster count. Evaluation alone ≈87.5 GPU-hours here or 36.2 effective H100 GPU-hours; extraction ≈12.8 GPU-hours, plus predictor runs and zoo creation. 1,000 entries are not necessarily 1,000 independent clusters: count them explicitly. Extend split manifests and dataset validation in `splits.py` and `data.py` before collection.
