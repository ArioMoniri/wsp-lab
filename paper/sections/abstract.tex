Can weight changes predict how a model's measured capabilities change, without running the edited model? Takehi et al. report that a small, targeted edit to Qwen3.5-0.8B raised a benchmark score without gradients \citep{takehi2026wsp}, which motivates the question but does not show that effects can be predicted across edits or models. We ran an exploratory comparison of weight-statistic and edit-metadata predictors on 345 matched-reference rows from Qwen3.5-0.8B edits, with three seeds and family, seed and trajectory splits. The targets are fast-tier MMLU-Pro and HellaSwag accuracies, and neither is qualified as a predictor target. On the trajectory split, a matched M1 predictor reached mean Spearman 0.45 and 0.46 on the two targets. Seed-0 StatNN reached 0.57 and 0.65, and a descriptor-only model with no weight statistics reached 0.52 and 0.61 on the same seed and split. In these comparisons, edit metadata ranks edits nearly as well as StatNN, so much of the rank StatNN recovers is also available from metadata. The original H1 criterion was not demonstrated, H2 and H3 remain unresolved, and the suite does not test H4--H6. The three-seed results are exploratory, the alignment sub-study has not been run, and the conclusions cover the specific models, seed-0 baselines and accuracy endpoints reported here. The paper concerns prediction and auditing, not gradient-free optimization or a new safety-editing method.
