### Hypothesis, estimand and bias

Remedy (d) leaves the constructed R, scored realised increment, first-order predictor `⟨g, D_eff − R_eff⟩`, selected components, resolution rule, thresholds and ordered decision function unchanged. It changes eligibility for the primary contrast and therefore the population entering that contrast. Accept it as an exclusion-rule amendment, not as an unchanged operational test or a restoration of realised spectrum matching. “Spectrum-matched” must remain qualified **pre-rounding**. The 40/40 figure is a projection until every F10 check has actually run.

Writing `R_eff = R + E_R`, the contribution `−⟨g,E_R⟩` is already included in the registered forecast. This does not price all response curvature or prove absence of bias. Fresh rotation rounding is asymmetric, depends on W and R, and can correlate with the field or prediction signs even though the constructor does not take g as input. Extra energy can produce a negative offset in edit-minus-rotation response under a positive, approximately isotropic curvature model; arbitrary curvature and orientation do not guarantee its sign. The note's constant-sign argument is a useful nuisance mechanism, not a theorem about every pair.

F10.5's log-energy covariate, fixed tertile reports, the pre-listed 30- and 34-pair sensitivities and constant-sign comparator are appropriate disclosure. The 30-pair subset removes ten low-tertile edits; it is not an independent confirmation. Covariate adjustment and those subsets do not certify an unbiased primary contrast, repair its null empirically, or authorize choosing another primary denominator after outcomes. Agreement above chance does not establish superiority over the constant-sign comparator or isolate directional geometry from quantisation/curvature. This is compatible with A5.3's limited claim. Condition 6 carries these limits into execution and reporting.

### Three implementability edits

**F10.3(iv), one-fp32-spacing slack: CONFIRM, with executable verification required.** The registered operation is fp32 addition followed by bf16 rounding. The intermediate rounding can land on a bf16 midpoint and select the neighbour farther from the exact sum. A single-rounding bound alone can reject correct code. I independently ran a CPU boundary example in memory with W = 1 and fp32 R = `2^-8 + 2^-26`: the bound without slack fails and the bound with the stated fp32 slack passes. No synthetic artifact was written. The extra spacing is a conservative allowance for this fixed arithmetic, not a fitted noise tolerance. Do not silently replace the constructor by fp64 addition or a direct bf16 sum. Implement spacing by magnitude with explicit zero/subnormal handling and fail closed on nonfinite arithmetic; the exponent formulas describe normal values, not zero or subnormal spacing. Test signs, binade boundaries, midpoint ties and cancellation. The drafter's uncommitted verbal check is not implementation evidence.

**F10.3(i), near-cut rank budget: CONFIRM as the stated numerical tolerance, with a wording qualification and tests.** Rank is discontinuous: exact equality is an inappropriate requirement for a fixed fp32 rotation even when fp64 audits find tiny norm/spectrum errors. The explicit rule is `abs(rank_R − rank_D) <= N_near`, where `N_near = count(abs(σ_j(D) − 0.001 σ_1(D)) <= 0.00001 σ_1(D))`; each rank uses its own largest singular value and strict `>` at its cut. Keep this rule and the independent fp64 norm/spectrum/reconstruction/orthogonality checks fixed before re-stage yields are seen. A count budget is not proof that individual crossings were caused only by near-cut values; describe it as a budget, and log the ranks, cuts and N_near. The Frobenius spectrum criterion alone is not an entrywise `1e-5 σ_1` perturbation theorem. Do not widen the window or add ±1 after seeing failures. Tests must cover equality at the cut/window edges, both rank directions, own-σ1 thresholds, N_near = 0, and rejection above the budget.

**F10.3(iii), replay: CONFIRM.** It correctly specifies fresh seeded reconstruction under fixed code, environment and thread settings, followed by comparison against reloaded on-disk bytes. Recreate the initial generator state for each tensor, as A5.3.3 requires; do not advance one stream across tensors. Compare roots' realised delta and chains' incremental file to the replay. Separately reload the written cumulative delta and verify that scorer arithmetic at the base reconstructs the intended successor, with unedited predecessor changes carried unchanged. Comparing cumulative chain deltas directly with predecessor-relative R_eff would be wrong. Use bit-pattern comparison, not merely floating equality if the report claims bitwise identity. Pin environment versions and thread settings sufficiently to reproduce QR/matmul, and retain mismatch tests. The current implementation does not yet supply these F10 checks; acceptance of their specification is not acceptance of finished code.
