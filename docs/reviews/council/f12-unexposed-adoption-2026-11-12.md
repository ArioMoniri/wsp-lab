## Independent method checks and provenance risks

The studentiser algebra has an independent derivation. Let g_i = sign(a_i)/S_a − sign(b_i)/S_b and h_c = sum over i in c of [sign(a_i) q_ai/S_a − sign(b_i) q_bi/S_b]. The component/item cell influence is sum_i g_i D_ij − h_c. Expanding its weighted square gives the code's `s1 - 2*h_c*s2 + J*h_c**2`; the triangular pair weights (one diagonal, two off-diagonal) reproduce the full double sum. Component and item terms use their respective finite-sample factors, and the overlap term uses nJ/(nJ−1). This agrees with the older `tfv1_requalify.py:373–455` when kappa=1 and observed item weights are uniform. The targeted comparison tests this equality and the all-ones point's component variance. This is an algebraic adoption, not an appeal to the repaired smoke results. The variance floor is registered, but neither its conservatism nor coverage follows from this identity; F12c's qualification gates remain necessary.

For gamma, the denominator is a convex sum of absolute affine functions. Starting strictly below the target corresponds to a denominator strictly below its target level; any positive crossing of that higher level lies on the increasing side. With a finite bracket and positive numerator the root is unique, so the first grid crossing and bisection do not need a global monotonicity gate. The independent validation stream remains essential. Diagnostic monotonicity tolerance does not choose governed eligibility under `convex` mode.
