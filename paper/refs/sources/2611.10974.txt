Corresponding author: zhangyue@westlake.edu.cn Abstract Reasoning in large language models can be disrupted by simple input perturbations (e.g. adding irrelevant distractors), suggesting reliance on spurious correlations. Identifying and suppressing the spurious features involved in long chain-of-thought reasoning remains challenging. We introduce Spurious-feature WEight EdiTing (SWEET), a training-free method that combines counterfactual interventions with mechanistic interpretability to identify and suppress spurious-features. SWEET extracts token- and sequence-level behavioral directions from activation contrasts between original problems and their counterfactual variants—capturing local and global patterns of spurious reasoning. It then localizes the responsible MLP neurons and attention heads and suppresses their contributions along these directions through weight edits. Using only 20 original–counterfactual pairs for direction extraction, SWEET improves accuracy by an average of 11.8 percentage points on perturbed inputs and 2 points on original inputs across five open-weight reasoning models and five reasoning benchmarks. It also reduces the relative gap between original and perturbed accuracies from 17.6% to 2.5%. Analyses identify two recurring forms of spurious reasoning, i.e. over-integrating irrelevant conditions and overthinking, and provide mechanistic evidence that SWEET improves reasoning robustness by suppressing both behavioral directions. Figure 1: Overview of SWEET. (a) SWEET constructs answer-preserving perturbations by adding irrelevant conditions, then selects original–perturbed pairs for which the model answers only the original problem correctly. (b) Token-level extraction contrasts activations along the same reasoning trace under both prompts, focusing on positions most affected by the perturbation to capture the incorporation of irrelevant information ( over-integration ). (c) SWEET ranks attention heads and MLP neurons by their contributions to these directions, selects the number of components to edit using calibration data, and modifies their output weights to remove contributions along the extracted directions. 1 Introduction Large language models (LLMs) have made remarkable progress on reasoning tasks ( OpenAI, 2025 ; Guo et al., 2025 ; Team et al., 2026 ) . However, even strong models can fail under simple perturbations, such as rephrasing problem scenarios, changing numerical values or adding distractors ( Huang et al., 2026 ; Mirzadeh et al., 2025 ) . This brittleness suggests reliance on spurious correlations ( Geirhos et al., 2020 ) , making performance sensitive to how problems are presented rather than solely to the reasoning required. As LLMs are increasingly used for scientific problem solving and safety-critical decision-making, such failures can undermine scientific conclusions and lead to harmful decisions. Prior work on mitigating spurious correlations has primarily focused on discriminative tasks, where models predict a single label ( Mahabadi et al., 2020 ; Wang et al., 2022 ; Zhou and Zhu, 2025 ) , rather than long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning ( Chen et al., 2026 ) . In long CoT, an error caused by a spurious feature at one step can propagate through the remaining trace, obscuring the source of the final failure. Recent methods address spurious-feature reliance in CoT through token pruning and distillation or causal interventions ( Wu et al., 2024 ; Xie et al., 2025 ; Guo et al., 2026 ) . Although these methods improve accuracy, such gains alone do not establish which forms of spurious-feature reliance, if any, have been mitigated. Without identifying the behaviors and internal components responsible for spurious reasoning, it remains unclear whether interventions correct the underlying failure mechanisms or compensate for their effects ( Mendelson and Belinkov, 2021 ) . To bridge this gap, our central intuition is that identifying and characterizing spurious features not only provides an interpretable account of reasoning failures, but also enables us to directly suppress their influence, thereby increasing the contribution of causally relevant features and improving reasoning robustness. We introduce Spurious-feature WEight EdiTing (SWEET), a training-free method for identifying and suppressing spurious-feature reliance in LLM reasoning. Motivated by Pearl’s causal hierarchy ( Pearl, 2009 ) , we treat answer-preserving perturbations as counterfactual interventions on task-irrelevant aspects of the input (Figure 1 (a)). We contrast paired reasoning activations at token and sequence levels to extract behavioral directions associated with local and global patterns of spurious reasoning, respectively (Figure 1 (b)). We then localize responsible attention heads and MLP neurons and remove their contributions along these directions through weight edits (Figure 1 (c)). The edited model can be applied to subsequent problems without further modification or training, with improved robustness to spurious features. To the best of our knowledge, SWEET is the first method to combine counterfactual interventions with mechanistic interpretability to identify and suppress spurious-feature reliance at both token and sequence levels in long-CoT reasoning. We evaluate SWEET on five open-weight reasoning models across five reasoning benchmarks and their distractor-augmented variants. Using up to 20 original–perturbed pairs to extract behavioral directions, SWEET improves accuracy by an average of 11.8 percentage points on perturbed inputs and 2 points on original inputs, while reducing the accuracy gap between original and perturbed inputs from 17.6% to 2.5%. These results demonstrate SWEET’s effectiveness across model families, scales, and reasoning benchmarks. We further analyze the extracted behavioral directions to characterize spurious reasoning and understand SWEET’s robustness gains. Across five models, we identify two recurring patterns: over-integration , in which models incorporate solution-relevant conditions and irrelevant distractors into their reasoning without distinguishing between them, and overthinking , in which models repeatedly question, revise, or abandon otherwise viable reasoning steps. Causal interventions show that editing the corresponding directions suppresses these behaviors, providing mechanistic evidence that SWEET improves robustness by reducing these two forms of spurious reasoning. Interpreting behavioral directions through vocabulary projections. Following Appendix G , we project the extracted directions through the unembedding matrix and rank tokens by their mean raw projection scores (Table 2 ). TL readouts include additive and premise-introducing terms such as also , additionally , given , mention , and their Chinese counterparts. Since the perturbations introduce irrelevant conditions, these readouts motivate the hypothesis that TL captures their incorporation into reasoning, which we term over-integration . SL readouts are noisier and less interpretable. Logit-lens interpretations can be brittle ( Belrose et al., 2023 ) , particularly for SL directions, which contrast mean-pooled states rather than states at a shared next-token position. We therefore treat the link between SL and overthinking as a behavioral hypothesis and test it below. TL and SL have complementary effects on reasoning robustness. We compare SL, TL, and SL+TL on original and NoOp inputs, assessing over-integration and overthinking separately (Table 3 ). For over-integration, a blinded GPT-6-Luna judge compares Base and edited traces for the same NoOp problem in both presentation orders. We count a win (loss) when both orders indicate less (more) over-integration in the edited trace, reporting rates over all evaluated trace pairs. We complement these judgements with lexical reuse: the relative change in the fraction of responses that repeat wording from the irrelevant condition, detected through rule-based 3-gram matching. Appendix E provides further details and discussion of these measures. We assess overthinking through generation length, token efficiency, token-cap incidence ( Kaiser et al., 2026 ) , and repetition-loop incidence in length-matched prefixes ( Alimaskina et al., 2026 ) , with metric definitions in Appendix F . TL reduces both over-integration and overthinking on NoOp inputs. Its over-integration win/loss rates are 51.6 % / 4.4 % 51.6\%/4.4\% , compared with 26.4 % / 20.2 % 26.4\%/20.2\% for SL, indicating a stronger reduction in over-integration. Lexical reuse shows a corresponding difference: TL reduces the fraction of responses repeating distractor wording by 77.5 % 77.5\% , compared with 0.6 % 0.6\% for SL. Other LLM judges support the same qualitative conclusion (Appendix E.3 ). On original inputs, where no irrelevant condition is added, TL’s effect on overthinking is much weaker: token efficiency changes by − 0.5 % -0.5\% , compared with + 56.1 % +56.1\% on NoOp, and reductions in generation length and token-cap incidence are smaller than for SL. This pattern suggests that TL primarily targets over-integration and that its reduction of overthinking on NoOp may partly reflect reduced incorporation of distractors. Attention analysis further shows that TL-active positions attend strongly to distractors (Appendix D ). SL reduces overthinking on both original and NoOp inputs, with token-efficiency gains of 10.4 % 10.4\% and 26.6 % 26.6\% , respectively. Although its NoOp gains are smaller than TL’s, its benefits on original inputs support the hypothesis that SL addresses broader overthinking tendencies. Combining the directions gives the strongest improvements across all measured metrics on NoOp and the largest reductions in generation length, token-cap incidence, and repetition loops on original inputs. These results support combining TL and SL to suppress both forms of spurious reasoning. SWEET’s robustness gains extend beyond mitigating overthinking. Bold marks the highest accuracy in each input condition. 6 Related Work Mitigating spurious correlations. Work on spurious correlations has predominantly focused on natural language understanding, using ensembles ( Mahabadi et al., 2020 ) , token masking ( Wang et al., 2022 ) , data augmentation ( Korakakis et al., 2025 ) , causal reweighting ( Zhou and Zhu, 2025 ) , or neuron pruning based on Integrated Gradients ( Ali et al., 2025 ) . These methods target shortcuts in classification, leaving unclear how to identify and suppress spurious dependencies throughout long CoT reasoning. Reasoning-specific methods address this challenge through training or reasoning-path selection: LeaF combines confounding-token pruning with attention distillation ( Guo et al., 2026 ) , DeCoT uses causal interventions to select reasoning paths ( Wu et al., 2024 ) , and Xie et al. (2025) use correctness-perception distillation to reduce spurious question–answer correlations. SWEET intervenes directly in the model’s internal computation: it identifies attention heads and MLP neurons associated with spurious-feature dependence and edits their weights to suppress their contributions—without retraining the model or adding reasoning-path selection at inference time. Analyzing and modifying model behavior. Activation steering ( Turner et al., 2023 ; Rimsky et al., 2024 ) and parameter interventions have been used to study and alter model behavior, including safety ( Wei et al., 2024 ) and CoT faithfulness ( Tutek et al., 2025 ) . ThinkEdit ( Sun et al., 2025 ) and Steer2Edit ( Sun et al., 2026 ) perform component-level weight editing using directions derived from sequence-averaged activation contrasts. In contrast to these methods, SWEET combines answer-preserving counterfactual interventions with token- and sequence-level extraction to distinguish local responses to irrelevant information from changes across the reasoning trajectory. Token-level extraction compares activations along the same reasoning trace under the original and perturbed prompts, whereas sequence-level extraction compares mean activations across independently generated traces. Together, these contrasts identify components associated with distinct forms of spurious reasoning, and guide weight edits that suppress their contributions. 7 Conclusion We introduced SWEET, a training-free method that combines mechanistic interpretability with counterfactual interventions to identify spurious-feature reliance in CoT reasoning at both token and sequence levels and suppress it through weight edits. Across five open-weight reasoning models and five benchmarks, SWEET improves average accuracy on both original and perturbed inputs while substantially reducing the relative accuracy gap between them. Detailed analyses provide mechanistic evidence that these robustness gains stem from suppressing two recurring forms of spurious reasoning: over-integration and overthinking . Limitations Our study has three limitations. First, although we use widely adopted benchmarks, these provide only a partial assessment of reasoning robustness: our evaluation focuses on irrelevant-information perturbations in mathematical reasoning, leaving applicability to other tasks and perturbation types unverified. Second, mitigating spurious-feature reliance can involve trade-offs between robustness and task accuracy. Although SWEET improves accuracy on both original and perturbed inputs on average, editing can reduce performance on some model–dataset combinations. Further work is needed to understand when these trade-offs arise and which capabilities are affected. Third, SWEET requires calibration data and additional computation to select editing sparsities, although this procedure is less computationally demanding than training-based mitigation. We use a common selection procedure across models, keep the selected settings fixed across held-out datasets, and demonstrate robustness across the tested sparsity settings.
