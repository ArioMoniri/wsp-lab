A.1. Baseline Methods
Weight-space PCA. We perform PCA on the standardized training set {θ̃i }N  i=1 , retaining the top-k principal components
Uk ∈ Rdθ ×k and representing each parameter vector by its projection zi = Uk⊤ θ̃i .

Rdz → Rdθ with a Gaussian reparameterized latent z = µϕ (θ̃) + σϕ (θ̃) ⊙ ϵ, ϵ ∼ N (0, I), using the VAE objective (Kingma
& Welling, 2014)

Setting β = 0 gives the deterministic autoencoder (AE); β = 1 gives the standard VAE. In both cases, the embedding of
point i is the encoder mean zi = µϕ (θ̃i ), and no coupling information is used during training.

Conditional generation. At inference, all three baselines introduce coupling information through a shared post-hoc step:
we fit a 2-layer MLP from couplings to the learned weight embeddings. Given a new coupling g ⋆ , we predict ẑ(g ⋆ ) and
apply the corresponding inverse to recover parameters.
In contrast, JEPAWG requires no post-hoc fit: the coupling encoder Eg and predictor P are trained jointly with the weight
encoder via the VICReg invariance, so the learned composition P ◦ Eg plays the role of the post-hoc regressor used by the
baselines.

B. Data Generation
The dataset analyzed in this work is a collection of trained network parameters of the coupling-conditional flow of
Section 3.1, each near-optimal as a sampler for the two-dimensional ϕ4 lattice field theory at a different point of the coupling
space g = (m2 , λ). We generate it in two stages: a coupling-conditional pre-training that produces a single model covering
the whole coupling box (Section B.1), followed by per-point fine-tuning on a regular grid. The output is an N × N array of

D.1. Derivation of the FSS partner map
Near a continuous phase transition, finite-size scaling predicts that observables depend primarily on the scaling combination

D.2. Critical-line estimation
The FSS matching procedure requires an estimate of the critical line m2c (λ). We consider two estimators:

  1. χ-peak fit: for each λ we locate the maximum of the magnetic susceptibility χ along the m2 direction (with parabolic
     refinement) to obtain a pseudo-critical mass, average over lattice sizes, and fit the result with a low-order polynomial
     m2c (λ).
  2. U4 crossing estimator: the critical mass is obtained from Binder-cumulant crossings between neighboring lattice sizes.

These estimators differ in their finite-size corrections and therefore provide a robustness check for the latent-space FSS
analysis.

G. Shuffled Data Control Experiment
To verify that JEPAWG recovers coupling structure from the flow weights rather than exploiting architectural inductive
biases, we train three shuffled-label controls. In each, the 361 fine-tuned grid points are randomly permuted before training
so that the encoder sees a broken correspondence between couplings and weights. Global shuffle: a uniformly random
permutation of all 361 points. Local shuffle k=5 / k=25: a greedy random matching in which each coupling is swapped
only with one of its k nearest neighbors in normalized (m2 , λ) space, limiting how far the pairing deviates from the true
layout. The permutation is fixed at the start of training and not re-drawn during training. Six independent shuffle seeds are
run per condition; the model initialization seed is held fixed across them.
All shuffled models reach the same training-time R2 ≈ 0.97 as the clean model, confirming sufficient capacity. To evaluate
whether the learned latent space encodes the true coupling, we apply each trained encoder to the original, unshuffled grid
points and measure R2 of a linear coupling predictor (Table 8). The global control collapses (R2 ≈ 0.08–0.10). The
local controls retain substantial signal that decreases monotonically with neighborhood radius: R2 ≈ 0.96 for k=5 and
R2 ≈ 0.91 for k=25, reflecting that very local swaps preserve much of the coupling–weight structure.
As a second check, we rerun the FSS ν scan on the shuffled embeddings (Figure 12). When we shuffle all the couplings,
the recovered exponent moves to the edge of the scan (ν ∗ ≈ 0.3). When we only swap nearby couplings, the encoder still
recovers the coupling and the exponent stays close to the unshuffled value.
