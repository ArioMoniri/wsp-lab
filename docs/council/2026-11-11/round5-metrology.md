## 1. One model or four?

Use one **forecast ledger and receiver-anchored local representation**, not one universal outcome model. For every candidate record the exact effective delta, its source/lineage, `a`, `q`, `κ`, scale, predicted signs and uncertainty/abstention before outcome access. The shared representation is the base receiver plus the proposed edit; prediction heads and scoring rules remain endpoint-specific. The existing G2 field, chart and TF-v1 can be reused for teacher-forced endpoints, but their labels do not measure termination, answer correctness or test-time adaptation (A5.8.1; `configs/eval_tf_v1_wikitext.yaml`; `docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, F8.5–F8.6).

JEPAWG-style latent dynamics add value only if there are enough *ordered, measured edit trajectories* to learn state transitions beyond the frozen first-order terms. They do not repair missing generation labels or make the present field a validated predictor. Keep trajectory learning as a later hypothesis; first establish forecast accuracy and endpoint reliability.

## 2. Forecast-before-modify log and calibration

Amend F9 to require an append-only, hash-pinned forecast row before scoring **every future eligible edit** (Part A variants excluded): base/endpoint/config hashes, source cluster, family, realised bf16 delta hash, `a`, `q`, `κ=q/|a|`, strength, `sign(a)`, quadratic-sign forecast/flip point, predicted interval if defined, abstention reason and timestamp. Record forecast sign and outcome sign with the same endpoint convention; zero realised changes remain disagreements per F8.3 (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, A5.3.2, F8.3).

Primary directional statistic: non-abstained sign agreement with a two-sided exact Clopper–Pearson interval, resampling/counting independent source clusters rather than duplicate edits. Report **coverage** as the fraction of all eligible entries for which the quadratic-sign rule gives a non-abstaining prediction, and conditional agreement separately. Secondary: Spearman correlation between the frozen signed linear forecast `a` and measured change, with a source-cluster bootstrap interval. Do not invent a continuous second-order forecast by assigning a sign to `q`: A5.8 defines `q` as a nonnegative magnitude, not a signed Hessian correction. A point sign is not a prediction interval: classical interval coverage is undefined unless a forecast interval and its construction are frozen in advance.

Log at any sample size; below 30 independent clusters per family, show entries only. At 30–99, label results exploratory and give exact intervals. Require at least 100 independent clusters per family before a precision-oriented sign-calibration statement; at worst-case accuracy 0.5, 100 gives an approximate 95% margin of 0.10 (exact intervals govern). A statement that the forecast is better than chance additionally requires the exact interval's lower bound above 0.5. The current F8 source-clustered panel is explicitly too small for inference (`docs/council/2026-11-11/SYNTHESIS.md`, §1, F8.1).

## 3. Sparsity ladder and 64-window resolution

Register nine outcome-blind masks: Wanda, SparseGPT and count-matched random at 10%, 30% and 50% unstructured sparsity, using fixed support/seed recipes. Freeze the field's ranking before pruning outcomes; report `a`/`q` rankings against measured WikiText changes, but call the nine-row comparison a descriptive screen, not proof of rank calibration. A sparsity fraction is not an expected perplexity effect. The pruning study concerns reasoning/test-time-scaling performance on different models; PPF's mask-to-performance surrogate is likewise not evidence that our first-order field ranks masks on this receiver ([arXiv:2604.25098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25098); [arXiv:2508.02381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02381)).

The registered WikiText endpoint is 64 × 1,024-token windows (`configs/eval_tf_v1_wikitext.yaml`). Its D3 qualification reports an upper 97.5% panel-mean half-width of 0.00487 nats and a maximum per-entry half-width of 0.01282 nats (`docs/reviews/P5/tf-v1-requalification-2026-11-06.md`). These correspond to about 0.49% and 1.29% relative perplexity, respectively, by exponentiating log-PPL. This is the observed resolution anchor, not a guarantee for new masks. It says nothing about whether 10%, 30% or 50% pruning causes an effect of that size; only scored, paired intervals answer that.

Warm TF-v1 scoring measured at most 1.040 min per entry (`docs/RESULTS.md`, “TF-v1 pilot round 3”). Nine masks plus an unscored/reused base reference are 9.36 min = **0.156 GPU-h**; if the base must be scored, 10.4 min = **0.173 GPU-h**. Quote **0.17 GPU-h scoring-only**; mask construction, independent repeats, qualification, cache/load overhead and retries must be timed and repriced. This small panel cannot establish a predictive ranking.

## 4. Self-localisation

Use probe/attribution localisation as the editor's reference input, with verbal self-localisation logged as a separately randomized treatment—not as ground truth. OWMI found chance-level verbal reports but recoverable intervention information in activations/probes across its tested models, so require a direct incremental test rather than trusting testimony ([arXiv:2608.20569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20569)). On matched proposals and budgets, compare probe-only against probe-plus-verbal-location on held-out evaluation support, charge all editor/probe/evaluation cost, and report each treatment flag. This is an H5 experiment, not evidence for the F8 panels; the controls and held-out acceptance rules already exist (A5.8.3; F5.4).

## 5. Cheapest decisive critic-versus-evaluation cost test

Use the **existing F8.7/G5 stream and cap**, not a new editor, model scale or endpoint. Freeze each critic forecast before evaluation, then compare the critic-gated scheduler with its matched no-critic after-the-fact evaluation policy at equal information and proposal opportunity. Apply F5.4's required random best-of-N comparison for any editor-advantage claim and A5.7's paired realised-savings, noninferiority, missed-winner, power and net-cost conditions; the ≥30% savings threshold is unchanged (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, F5.4, A5.7, F8.7; `docs/council/2026-11-11/SYNTHESIS.md`, §1).

Do not call an offline replay decisive: evaluating every skipped candidate to discover missed winners spends the very evaluations whose savings are being claimed. Replay already-scored proposals first as a zero-new-GPU screen; then the cheapest *decisive* claim is the smallest prospective matched stream that meets A5.7's frozen power and missed-winner requirements. The H5 design gives 7.5 min/entry as a planning anchor, not a verified cost for this future run; count actual full-evaluation, forecast, diagnostic, failure and retry costs, and reprice before launch (`docs/H5_DESIGN.md`, “Editor arms, controls, and cost accounting”; `docs/H5_PROTOCOL.md`, 11 Nov amendment). No fixed candidate count or GPU-hour can be honestly asserted without the power inputs and pilot timing.

## 6. F8-E generation endpoint

Add a **descriptive F8-E-only** panel for the exact published Astra edit, not a replacement for TF-v1 or a new F8-F pruning arm. Use the same 500 MATH-500 problems and paired item/seed conditions for (i) unedited thinking-on, (ii) edited thinking-on, and (iii) unedited thinking-off. Fix an 8,192-token cap and decoding settings in advance; report natural EOS completion, strict MATH correctness and cap-hit rate separately, with correctness conditionality disclosed. Use the same seed across paired arms; the independent replication used temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20 and presence penalty 1.5, and explicitly separated completion from correctness ([Krishanu Das replication](https://dev.to/krishanu_das/before-trusting-a-weight-edit-i-measured-the-bug-it-fixes-401f)). It found 2/30 versus 10/30 natural completions and 1/30 versus 9/30 correct at 8,192 tokens, but its Holm-adjusted result was not significant; that motivates measurement, not an effect-size prior.

For an initial screen, collect **five independent paired seeds per item per arm** (7,500 generations total). Sampling qualification needs two independent five-seed batches per item/arm (10 samples/item; 15,000 generations total), plus a same-device, same-seed replay to check deterministic repeatability; use item-and-seed-paired uncertainty and an A3-style assessment of panel-mean half-width, repeat-error 95th percentile and reliability. Freeze a meaningful margin `δ` for each rate before sampling and require the A2.1-style upper 95% uncertainty bound and repeat-error bound ≤ `δ/2`, with reliability lower bound ≥0.90; otherwise label the endpoint unqualified and retain descriptive intervals only (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, A2.1 and A3; `docs/reviews/P5/tf-v1-requalification-2026-11-06.md`). Use the same predeclared MATH-500 answer parser across arms, independent of whether the response contains a thinking-tag closure. Ten samples/item does not itself guarantee qualification.

At 8,192 tokens, the full qualification panel has a maximum output budget of **122.88 million generated tokens** (500 × 10 × 3 × 8,192); actual length is lower when EOS occurs. `docs/RESULTS.md` has no comparable generation-throughput measurement, so converting this to GPU-hours would be invented. Time a small same-device pilot and extrapolate from its per-arm token/s distribution, including prompt prefill, reloads and repeats, before authorizing the full panel. This endpoint can **never** change F5.5 or become confirmatory under current F8: F8.6 bars these panels from satisfying, failing or unresolving the line-stop. A later capability claim would need its own prospective registration, qualified sampling, disjoint items, multiplicity and power—not relabel this descriptive result.
