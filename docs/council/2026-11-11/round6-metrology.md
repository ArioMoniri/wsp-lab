## 1. Is this a “single neuron”?

No. The primary forecast is a fixed functional of the exact realised delta \(D\): \(a=\langle g,D\rangle\), plus the K-FAC/Gauss–Newton magnitude \(q=\frac12\mathrm{tr}(GDAD^\top)\); the online head can fit at most an intercept and coefficients on \(a\) and \(q\) (A5.8.3; `docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, A5.4/F9.2). It spans all coordinates in the base model’s gradient, not one neuron. The linear contraction sums coordinate contributions and permits cancellation across tensors, but has no cross-tensor Hessian terms, state-conditioned interactions, or dependence on the path by which an edit was reached. \(q\) is a nonnegative magnitude, not signed curvature. A ray’s \(a\) keeps its sign as scale changes; the combined fitted forecast may cross zero via its quadratic term, but this is not a learned nonlinear ladder response.

For infinitesimal edits, the first-order term is the endpoint’s directional derivative; if higher-order terms are small, it can predict a finite edit’s local direction. It cannot promise the sign or magnitude of a large, non-local edit, a sign reversal along a ladder, or a trajectory-dependent effect without additional response data/modeling. The user’s “not a single neuron” premise is therefore right literally but sets up a false alternative: a single scalar readout can summarize a high-dimensional gradient, while remaining a restrictive local predictor.

The 33/33, 33/33 and 25/25 agreements are exact sign matches between the frozen 52-edit predictions and the previously generated power-simulation fields for the two ladders and rotation; there are nine rotation abstentions. They verify prediction construction/field alignment only, not outcome accuracy (`docs/prereg/a5_manifests/part_a/PRESCORE_INVENTORY_2.md`, §5; `docs/PROGRESS.md`, 11 Nov 10:58). F11.4’s 0.8722 is simulated ordered power for the supplemented design; it does not mean predictions were right on real edits (`docs/prereg/a5_manifests/part_a/f11_calibration_record.json`). Part A is explicitly staged and unscored; until scored, it establishes neither “single neuron” success nor failure, nor that a richer model wins (`docs/council/2026-11-11/BRIEF-r6.md`; `docs/prereg/a5_manifests/part_a/PRESCORE_INVENTORY_2.md`).

## 2. What would count as this model’s world model?

Operationally, \(f_\theta(\text{state},D)\) must be trained on this pinned base’s observed edit/outcome pairs and predict a defined endpoint change on an unseen source component. Merely using a model’s weights is not enough; the outcome-trained map makes it a response model.

## 3. Sample size, measured noise, and the census purchase

**No exact held-out row count follows from the supplied measurements.** The 0.00487-nat value is the upper 97.5% half-width for the qualified 64-window WikiText panel mean; 0.01282 nats is the maximum per-entry half-width (`docs/reviews/P5/tf-v1-requalification-2026-11-06.md`). Neither is the variance of paired out-of-fold absolute residuals, their covariance across predictors, or the between-component variance. Likewise, the 342/278 P4 counts and rank correlations do not supply those quantities. Converting any of them to an \(N\) for \(C=0.15\) would invent an effect/noise model.

The preregisterable answer is the **smallest held-out set of whole source components** whose development-only A5.4.2 simulation has ≥0.80 power at \(C=0.15\), for both the conditional and joint procedures. Build it from paired OOF row losses; retain component sizes, shared 64-window/item blocks, common references and their covariance; refit nuisance/predictors inside qualification simulations; resample whole components (and common item blocks jointly); use the registered 4,000-draw bootstrap-t ratio \(C=1-A_\text{learned}/A_\text{comparator}\), including the near-zero-denominator and failed-draw rules. Require the registered null-size, coverage and failure gates, and freeze the simulation before outcomes. Run separate contrasts against the field and StatNN; a “beats both” claim needs both powered, multiplicity handled in F12. This is the exact ratio—not a proxy correlation—that A5.4.2 requires.

The 565 remaining TF-v1 entries at 1.04 min each cost \(587.6\) min, or \(9.79\) GPU-h, scoring-only (`docs/RESULTS.md`, “TF-v1 pilot round 3”; census counts: `docs/council/2026-11-11/BRIEF-r6.md`). Closing the census buys more labels and wider edit/family/trajectory coverage, valuable for training and permitting whole-component holdouts. It does **not** buy 565 independent samples: current chain supervision is nested in 99 lineages, and the P4 evidence already shows 342 rows collapse to 278 ancestry clusters (`docs/PROGRESS.md`, 7 Nov 22:10; `docs/reviews/P4/d3v2f-summary-2026-11-08.md`). Run the CPU power simulation first; if it shows the extant clusters cannot detect \(C=0.15\), the census buys a chance to qualify the arm, not a guaranteed result. The additional scoring ceiling is 9.79 GPU-h; any activation passes and model training must be separately timed and repriced.

## 4. Model-specific by construction?

The field is already specific to the receiver because \(g,G,A\) are measured on this base, but that is not itself a learned response function. A5.4’s StatNN/GL-set arms qualify as model-specific world models only in the stronger supervised sense: they fit observed outcomes and are tested on held-out components. The planned field-only, augmented-StatNN, and field-absent contrasts already test whether field information improves an outcome-trained predictor; probe-response is a distinct, costed comparator (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, A5.4–A5.4.3). What is missing is a powered, prospective held-out comparison of one model-specific learner against both the field and StatNN at \(C=0.15\), with source and item dependence respected. Neither Part A’s small rotation/ladder experiment nor the 33/33 construction check supplies that test.

## 6. Which edit families expose the limitation?

Edit mechanisms change the distribution and scale of \(D\), not the mathematical definition of \(a\). A column edit or rank-one activation-derived edit is still forecastable by the contraction, but may stress locality and finite-step nonlinearity. Deletion masks are explicit deltas too; the existing F8-F has tiny deletion controls and F9-P registers a 10/20/50% mask ladder plus compensated SparseGPT, so deletion is covered, though not the learned mask regime (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, F8.2, F9.3). F8-E admits the released Astra external edit and, if released, the Exploring WSP column direction; F8-F includes TFTV/rank-one and a Concept Country-style activation edit. SWEET-style edits are deferred pending counterfactual-construction pricing; learned deletion masks are excluded as unpriced (`docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md`, F8.1–F8.2). Thus these are not all scored yet.

The most embarrassing cases are large finite edits with a scale-dependent response, and feedback-driven multi-step programs/policies whose next edit depends on prior outcomes: a base-anchored local functional has no trajectory state or policy. The external papers illustrate mechanisms, not predictive validation: Exploring WSP reports localized edits including a 1,023-parameter column and 16 trajectories, six retaining original weights ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382)); TFTV maps activation-steering vectors to rank-one edits ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09054)); SWEET derives directions from counterfactual activation contrasts ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10974)); Deletion Tuning learns masks ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10866)); WeightLoop uses experimental feedback to update an editing program ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07319)); RL model editing learns policies from reward feedback ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13461)). These make trajectory and amplitude controls important; they do not prove a first-order forecast fails on our registered endpoints.
