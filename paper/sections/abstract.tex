Can a model's weight changes predict how its measured capabilities change, without running the edited model? Recent work by Takehi et al. reports that a small, targeted edit to Qwen3.5-0.8B can improve a benchmark score without gradients \citep{takehi2026wsp}. That result motivates the question but does not show that such effects can be predicted across edits or models. We are preparing a study of performance prediction from weight deltas and edit trajectories. Concretely, it asks whether weight-only features predict per-benchmark changes, whether sequence models improve on predictors that treat each edit independently, whether a JEPA-style representation transfers to edit families it has not seen, and how an LLM reader compares with a trained predictor. A planned alignment sub-study compares instruction-tuned, directionally ablated, and Heretic-edited models on capability, refusal, and KL-to-base measures. None of these outcomes is established yet, and the hypotheses will be tested on a controlled edit zoo. The scope stays with prediction and auditing: we are not studying gradient-free optimization or proposing a new safety-editing method.
