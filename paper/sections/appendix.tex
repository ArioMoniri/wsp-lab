\section{Reproducibility statement}
\label{sec:repro}
% D8-VERBATIM-BEGIN
\textbf{Artefact loss.} On 6 November 2026 a version-control error deleted this project's working data directory (about 260 GB) before any off-machine backup of it existed (incident record: \texttt{docs/DECISIONS.md}, D8). The public Hugging Face dataset (\ifarxivpreprint\texttt{Aralario/wsp-lab-zoo}\else handle withheld for review\fi) preserved the zoo manifest and every edit record, the fast-tier evaluation summaries of all 389 evaluated entries (item-level records for 200 of them), both Lambda base evaluations, and the deltas of all 584 non-chain entries and of 317 of the 675 chain steps (254 stored, 63 byte-identical duplicates of stored deltas). The following artefacts were lost and are not released: (i) the deltas of 358 chain steps and everything derived from them; (ii) the run directories of the protocol-v2 predictor suite (\texttt{data/runs/p4/d3v2-*}: per-seed metric files, fold prediction tables, manifests); (iii) the per-item records, timing logs, KL base cache and qualification JSON of the TF-v1 pilot, G1, G2 and D3 stratum runs as originally produced; (iv) the item-level fast-tier records of the 189 locally evaluated entries (their summaries survive); (v) the original local base-model evaluation records. Where a number below was recomputed from regenerated artefacts, the text names the regeneration date and the source hash; regenerated values replace the originals wherever they differ, and the originals are listed in the appendix. Numbers marked ``reported from logs'' come from committed run summaries (\texttt{docs/RESULTS.md}, \texttt{docs/reviews/P4/}, \texttt{docs/reviews/P5/}) whose underlying artefacts no longer exist; they cannot be re-derived, and they are not used to support any claim stronger than descriptive.
% D8-VERBATIM-END

Regeneration is complete (report: \path{docs/reviews/P5/d8-regeneration-2026-11-06.md}). Every regeneration ran from a clean detached worktree at the commit that carries the recorded source hash, and each run checked its first record for that hash and a clean tree. The outcomes are as follows. Of the 1,259 manifest rows, 838 have delta bytes, 63 are byte-identical to a stored delta and 358 are lost; the 838 stored deltas show no hash, header or norm mismatch. Feature re-extraction covered 901 entries with no failure; no pre-incident feature file survives, so the new features cannot be compared with the old ones, and a ten-entry re-extraction in a fresh process was byte-identical. Three base re-evaluations under the three recorded source hashes (\texttt{d7cece35fad9}, \texttt{6f274a830401} and \texttt{e871923e1e18}) agree with every printed base value, and all 458 records agree across the source trees on every field they share; exact serialized identity across all three trees is not claimed, because the third tree adds a field to 100 generative records. The registered 229-row TF-v1 analysis was not regenerated and remains reported from logs. The seed-1 panel reproduces every printed value; the maximum absolute seed-to-seed KL difference is $3.3472\times10^{-4}$ nats, so KL is not seed-identical, and the statement in Section~\ref{sec:reliability} is the regenerated one. For TF-v1, the KL cache content root reproduced, 12 of 12 repeatable G1 entries were bitwise identical (2 of the original 14 are chain predecessors whose deltas are lost), all 11 rows of the G2 bridge reproduced, and all 28 recoverable rows of the requalification audit reproduced to the printed four digits. The round-1 timing pilot table was scored by a superseded scorer and is not a fingerprint of the frozen source; it is reported, not gated.

Results that depend on lost artefacts carry one of three labels. ``Reported from logs; artefacts lost'' marks a result whose artefacts no longer exist and whose printed value comes from a committed summary; it supports only descriptive claims. ``Regenerated on a stated date under the stated source hash, agreeing with the retained summary to the printed precision'' and ``Regenerated on a stated date, differing from the retained summary'' mark regenerated results. The seed-1 panel, the base re-evaluations and the TF-v1 fingerprints carry the first regenerated label; the KL seed sentence carries the second. The protocol-v2 table keeps its original run ids and is reported from logs: no original fold table survives, and the recovered-set rerun in Section~\ref{sec:v2} is a different experiment with fewer rows. The TF-v1 run ids in Section~\ref{sec:tfv1} name the regenerated runs.

The chain family is affected most. Of the 675 chain steps, 317 keep delta bytes and 358 do not. H2 is therefore not testable on the surviving zoo. Of the 99 lineages of length four or more, six keep their first four steps, and three of those six are evaluated. The registered 229-row TF-v1 panel cannot be reproduced without the lost chain deltas, and a recovered-set panel would be a different panel.

The incident review classifies every result group in the v2 paper, and Table~\ref{tab:d8-classes} gives the counts. Of the 21 B results, 15 depend on chain rows. The four C results are the protocol-v2 directory facts, the TF-v1 pilot timings, the D3 scoring minutes and the feature-manifest sentence. The first three are reported from logs; the feature-manifest claim is withdrawn and replaced in Section~\ref{sec:zoo} by the measured 901-entry extraction result, not reported from logs.
