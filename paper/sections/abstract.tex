Can a model's weight changes predict how its measured capabilities change, without running the edited model? Recent work by Takehi et al. shows that a small, targeted edit to Qwen3.5-0.8B can improve a benchmark score without gradients, motivating this question but not establishing that such effects can be predicted across edits or models \citep{takehi2026wsp}. We are preparing a study of performance prediction from weight deltas and edit trajectories. We will test whether weight-only features predict per-benchmark changes, whether sequence models improve on independent-edit predictors, whether a JEPA-style representation transfers to unseen edit families, and how an LLM reader compares with a trained predictor. A planned alignment sub-study will compare instruction-tuned, directionally ablated, and Heretic-edited models on capability, refusal, and KL-to-base measures. The study will evaluate these hypotheses on a controlled edit zoo; no outcome is yet established. Its scope is deliberately limited to prediction and auditing, not gradient-free optimization or a new safety-editing method.
