Can weight changes predict how a model's measured capabilities change, without running the edited model? Takehi et al. report that a small, targeted edit to Qwen3.5-0.8B raised a benchmark score without gradients \citep{takehi2026wsp}, which motivates the question but does not show that effects can be predicted across edits or models. We tested whether weight-only features and edit histories predict benchmark changes, using 345 matched-reference rows from Qwen3.5-0.8B edits, three seeds, and family, seed and trajectory splits. On the trajectory split, a matched M1 predictor reached mean Spearman 0.45 and 0.46 on MMLU-Pro and HellaSwag, and seed-0 StatNN reached 0.57 and 0.65. A descriptor-only model with no weight statistics reached 0.52 and 0.61, so much of the rank StatNN recovers is available from edit metadata. The original H1 criterion was not demonstrated, H2 and H3 remain unresolved, and the suite does not test H4--H6. The results are exploratory, the alignment sub-study has not been run, and no predictor target is qualified. The paper concerns prediction and auditing, not gradient-free optimization or a new safety-editing method.
