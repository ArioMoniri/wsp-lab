# Round 7 — metrology

## BLUF
1. WSR is positive evidence of a weight-access benefit in one small benchmark, not proof of generally reliable weight reading ([paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.weight-space-reading-language-models)).
2. Its 0.730 versus 0.578 balanced accuracy is a +0.152 difference, with reported 95% CI [+0.056, +0.256]; each cell ran once.
3. The subtle-edit result rests on 18 changed behavior labels from 10 interventions, not 18 independent edit replications.
4. Balanced accuracy usefully balances changed-label recall and unchanged-label specificity, but does not cure sparse, clustered labels.
5. Welch p < 0.01 plus a minimum effect defines labels; no multiplicity correction was used, and one run per cell leaves run variability unmeasured.
6. Muse's 53–88% change predictions on 40 inert edits warn that readers can over-predict changes, not just miss them.
7. Ahmed's separate audit supports dataset-prior caution, but is not a direct test of WSR's edit-prediction task ([audit](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08070)).
8. F12 was unresolved: its two-comparator decision fired in 218/1,000 simulations, with all 14 registered gates failing.
9. Develop a CPU-only scripted-statistics residual model first; do not buy scoring from the F12 result or treat WSR's text-only comparator as a control for computable statistics.
10. Set any future confirmatory sample size by a new outcome-blind, component-level power simulation; no defensible fixed N follows from the present evidence.

## WSR: what its evidence measures

The paper reports 0.730 balanced accuracy with weights and tools versus 0.578 with a text description on subtle, non-destructive edits to Qwen3.8-27B, a difference of +0.152 (95% CI [+0.056, +0.256]). Change recall increased from 0.278 to 0.667 while specificity fell from 0.878 to 0.793. This is a real, promising within-benchmark contrast, but evidence for a modest-sample access benefit rather than broad forecasting competence: the changed-label sample was 18 labels across 10 interventions, eligible dose variants may share parent edits, and each reader/target/condition cell ran once. The reported intervals therefore do not include run-to-run uncertainty. (Paper: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.weight-space-reading-language-models)

Balanced accuracy, the mean of change recall and specificity, is preferable to raw accuracy when unchanged labels dominate: an always-“same” rule scores 0.5. It does not make the 18 changed labels independent or stabilize behavior-specific estimates. The ground-truth rule uses Welch tests at p < 0.01 and minimum effects (10% of baseline tokens for thinking length, 0.05 for rates). That is a joint statistical/practical threshold, but tests across behaviors and edits received no multiplicity correction; thresholded “same” labels also inherit the noise and power of those tests. The paper's stronger recall comes with lower specificity, consistent with a reader more willing to call changes.

The Muse result is an important calibration check: none of 40 interventions changed measured behavior, yet readers predicted a change for 53–88%; Muse itself predicted one for 85%. Some uniform scaling edits may be neutralized by post-sublayer normalization, but the authors do not explain all inert edits. This is direct evidence that a reader's positive forecasts can reflect edit priors or over-prediction rather than weight-specific information. (Paper: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.weight-space-reading-language-models)

The text-only baseline controls for a template description, not for information an agent can extract from tensors using Python. The released harness explicitly gives its weights condition both weights and tools, while its text condition has neither; it describes no scripted-statistics reader arm. Thus +0.152 does not distinguish LLM weight reasoning from ordinary statistics computed on the weights. This missing comparator matters to the central interpretation. Ahmed's audit found local arithmetic alongside familiar-dataset semantic errors in small image classifiers, mostly single runs; it makes prior-driven interpretation plausible, but its task, model and data differ and it neither confirms nor refutes WSR's behavioral-edit result. (WSR protocol: https://github.com/suayptalha/wsr; Ahmed audit: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08070)

WSR's minimum effects are not commensurate with this programme's noise floor: WSR labels token-length shifts as a fraction of baseline tokens and rate shifts in absolute rate units; this programme predicts TF-v1 endpoint changes in nats per valid token. A5/F8's noise floor is h* = 0.0049397 nats/token, derived from paired panel-mean uncertainty, not a significance threshold for WSR's labels (PREREG_H1_TF.md, F8a.2 and subsequent floor reproduction; see also F8/F12 definitions). Neither the WSR p-value nor its 0.05 rate threshold can be imported as a TF-v1 resolution rule.

## Why F12 was underpowered

F12 was designed for 270 root rows in 234 source components and a learned residual model nested over the field; 123 existing roots and 147 candidate development roots formed that registered population (PREREG_H1_TF.md, F12.2; docs/prereg/f12_manifests/population.json). The decision required a ≥0.05 correction with positive conditional and joint lower bounds against both F3 and same-input StatNN. That conjunction paid for the small margin: under the registered alternative calibrated to C = 0.15 against F3, the simulated contrast was C_WM = 0.172 versus F3 but only C_WM,S = 0.045 versus StatNN. The model can be meaningfully better than the field while having little room over the already strong matched-information tree.

The report's ordered decision fired 218/1,000 times (one-sided lower bound 0.180, versus the required 0.80). All 14 gates failed: the five stress bounds and eight coverage qualifications exceeded 0.07, alongside the power failure. In the linear-signal/ridge-bias stress scenario, the ordered decision fired 110/1,000. These results identify weak sensitivity and interval qualification under this registered design; they do not establish that no K+F correction exists. Board A accepted “unresolved — not powered / not qualified,” with no purchase, extension, repeat, alternative k or new target authorized (docs/reviews/council/board-a-f12-sim-report-2026-11-14.md; PREREG_H1_TF.md, F12 chair acknowledgement).

The number of rows is not the effective sample size: folds and uncertainty resample source components (and shared item blocks), while rows within a component are related. Requiring two comparator wins compounds the problem; the narrow observed edge over StatNN makes a second positive lower bound especially hard. Ridge-bias stress and undercoverage further mean that simply adding rows under the same interval procedure is not a qualified remedy. The F12 simulation is the proper pricing precedent, not a basis for extrapolating a required N from 218/1,000.

## Development recommendation and prospective power gate

**Develop one scripted-statistics residual predictor, not an LLM reader first.** For each non-Part-A root edit, compute a frozen, CPU-only vector from the realized delta and existing field tokens: field terms (a, q), per-role/layer delta norms and relative norms, sparsity/rank summaries, and alignment/probe summaries where already available. Fit a low-complexity ridge residual head to TF-v1 ΔNLL after the field prediction, with all normalization and ridge selection inside source-component-grouped training folds. Predict the continuous endpoint in nats/token; report sign as a derived, non-primary view. Freeze feature definitions before fitting. This directly tests whether weight-derived statistics add information without confusing LLM reasoning with tool use.

Use only already-observed non-Part-A development roots for exploratory, component-held-out development; make no confirmatory claim from these reused outcomes. PREREG_H1_TF F12.2 records 123 existing roots/89 components, including 44 roots in 37 components marked confirmatory. Under the literal status exclusion, that leaves 79 roots/52 components (123−44 rows; 89−37 components), before any additional exposure exclusions. Do not score new rows or access Part A or `data/evals`. A later confirmatory test requires a new dated amendment and a new prospective panel, after Part A in the registered order and only on the programme owner's decision; F12's failed qualification authorizes no remedy.

Compare on identical folds and information: (1) F3 field, primary comparator; (2) residual ridge candidate; (3) same-input StatNN; (4) scripted-statistics-only predictor without outcome-fitted residual correction; and (5) fixed text-only description baseline. The primary estimand should be the candidate's held-out absolute-error reduction over F3. Report StatNN and the two reader controls as prespecified secondary contrasts, with their multiplicity handling frozen in the amendment—not as a second conjunctive gate unless the new simulation demonstrates adequate power for that exact decision.

There is no defensible numerical sample size today. Before any purchase, build an outcome-blind simulator on the actual prospective feature matrix and component/item structure. Vary the number of **independent source components** (not raw rows); refit the complete nested pipeline, including feature scaling and ridge selection, on every replicate. Include a null, an alternative calibrated to C = 0.15 against F3 (with the decision threshold fixed separately at C = 0.05), ridge-bias stress and a variance-×2 scenario; assess conditional and joint intervals with shared-item resampling. Choose the smallest prospective N whose exact one-sided binomial upper bound for null false detections and lower-/upper-tail coverage-error rates is ≤0.07, and whose lower bound for ordered power is ≥0.80 under the C = 0.15 alternative. Use enough simulation replicates to certify those bounds, not only point estimates; price the fixed design with a CPU timing pilot before full simulation. If no feasible N passes, do not purchase or enlarge the panel opportunistically. This is a new design, not an extension or rescue of F12.

Exploratory fitting and the power simulation require CPU only; use cached feature artifacts, with **0 GPU-h and 0 LLM tokens** for this development stage. CPU-h is unmeasured and must be obtained from a bounded pilot before the simulator runs. Prospective TF-v1 scoring requires GPU; its cost is unpriced until the selected N and timed scoring pilot are fixed. Kill the project before scoring if the outcome-blind simulation cannot certify the gates at an available, prospectively registered component count. Do not change F8/F9, H5 or F12-I; any change requires its own dated amendment before outcomes.
