## Audit-script assessment

`build_d8` is a useful committed-document consistency check, but its assertions do not establish all the sentences they appear to cover:

- The regeneration checks search the prose of `d8-regeneration-2026-11-06.md`; they do not load `base_compare.json`, `seed1_compare.json`, `g1_report.json`, `g2_compare.json`, `tf_yh_audit.json`, `feature_root.json` or `artefact_restore_summary.json`. A report sentence can pass even if the corresponding JSON changes or contradicts it.
- The KL rule searches for `up to 3.3e-4` in the report. It neither compares the actual JSON maximum (`3.347185e-4`) with the paper's `0.0003` bound nor tests an inequality. Thus the current false “at most 0.0003” sentence passes.
- The zoo feature rule only checks for the old `at most 901 (838 + 63 aliases)` wording in the incident plan. It does not consult the completed `feature_root.json`; consequently it permits the stale “until re-extraction finishes” sentence.
- The P4 rules parse selected rows of the recovered summary and check selected printed cells, ranges and `m3-family`. They do not read `d3v2-summary-2026-11-05.md`, compare the two row sets/model orderings, or assert the broad “same ordering” sentence. Claims registered with `values=None` are presence/accounting checks, not numerical or relational tests.
- The class-C check accounts for the number 4, not whether all four items are correctly described as “reported from logs.” The P1 exact-byte nuance is likewise not checked by the prose regex.

Keep the checker, but strengthen it to load the committed JSON evidence for exact counts and numeric comparisons, add explicit checks for `max(KL) <= displayed_bound` (or avoid the bound wording), and compare recovered/original ordering only if the sentence is explicitly scoped to a common split and a stated comparison. Do not treat passing this checker as closure approval.
