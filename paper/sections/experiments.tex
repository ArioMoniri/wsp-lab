\section{Experiments}
The subject is Qwen3.5-0.8B with thinking enabled. Fixed subsets cover GSM8K, MATH-500, IFEval, MMLU-Pro and HellaSwag. We also measure WikiText perplexity, refusal on harmful prompts, over-refusal on harmless prompts, and KL to the base. The cap-hit share is the fraction of responses pooled across GSM8K, MATH-500 and IFEval that reach the token limit without EOS (1,024 tokens at the fast tier, 4,096 at the cheap tier). It is a truncation proxy, not a validated detector of semantic repetition. Refusal keyword matching is a surface measure. It does not establish harmfulness.

The fast tier uses 50 GSM8K, 25 MATH-500, 25 IFEval, 100 MMLU-Pro and 100 HellaSwag items, plus 50 prompts each for refusal, over-refusal and KL. Generation is capped at 1024 tokens. At these sizes, a single binomial score for GSM8K, MATH-500 or IFEval has a standard error of 0.05 to 0.10 at the base fast-tier values (Section~\ref{sec:reliability}). GSM8K, MATH-500 and IFEval are therefore reported as descriptive at this tier. MMLU-Pro, HellaSwag, WikiText perplexity and KL are candidate predictor targets, but none is qualified until the criteria in Section~\ref{sec:reliability} are met. Measuring their floors is the first of those steps.

The cheap tier uses the larger fixed battery with a 4096-token limit, except for MMLU-Pro, which has a 1024-token limit. A faithful 32768-token tier is reserved for the base and at most 10 selected entries. The cheap tier has been run only on the base model (Table~\ref{tab:base-repro}), and the zoo evaluation configs record a 1,024-token cap. A timing measurement and coverage beyond the D3 sample have not been run. D3 drew production entries without replacement, using seed 0 and sorted IDs, with quotas of 60 random direction, 100 abliteration, 60 LoRA SFT, 30 task vector and 50 chain steps. Where a family fell short, D3 would take every available entry. On the current manifest (Table~\ref{tab:zoo-counts}), these quotas resolve to 300 entries, and all 300 were evaluated. Sampling settings and fixed item IDs are recorded in the evaluation configuration.

Table~\ref{tab:base-repro} places the completed cheap-tier base run beside the base values that the blog reports \citep{takehi2026wsp}. The blog's budgets, sample counts and loop definition differ, so these figures are not matched estimates. \subsection{Predictor suite under protocol v2}
\label{sec:v2}
We ran the predictor suite under protocol v2, with three seeds per run. Protocol v2 adds nested model selection, train-only preprocessing and paired bootstrap intervals over ancestry clusters. The matched set has 345 rows from 389 evaluated entries; the other 44 have no matched predecessor target and are excluded. The run files mark 260 of the 345 rows as having inferred runtime metadata, backfilled from job logs. Those 260 are the matched D3 edits counted in Section~\ref{sec:reliability}. The other 85 rows are chain predecessors, and neither the row nor its matched reference carries the backfill flag. The 345 rows are not the 300-entry D3 subset. Twenty-five run directories are reported as complete, from retained summaries (artefacts lost); the run ids in Table~\ref{tab:v2-suite} name directories that no longer exist. The directory \texttt{d3v2-m3-family} has a configuration and a split plan but no metrics, so we do not report it.

M1 is the stateless tensor-token predictor and M2 is the state-space sequence predictor. M1-matched and M2-matched are matched-input controls. M3 and M6 use JEPA-style objectives, and M5 is a causal trajectory transformer.

Both targets are fast-tier accuracies scored by answer-option likelihood: MMLU-Pro by the unnormalized log-probability of the answer letter, and HellaSwag by each ending's log-likelihood divided by its character count. \subsection{Pre-registered teacher-forced study}
\label{sec:tfv1}
\sloppy
The pre-registered test of H1a runs on a teacher-forced tier, TF-v1, specified in \path{docs/PREREG_H1_TF.md}. The candidate endpoint is the gold-letter log-probability on fixed MMLU-Pro items, scored under the edited model against its actual predecessor. The tier scores the likelihood of fixed answers, so it generates no text. As built, it scores 1,000 MMLU-Pro items, 250 HellaSwag items (a nested prefix of a 500-item ranking), 200 KL prompts with 64 greedy tokens each over the full vocabulary, 64 WikiText windows, 50 harmful prompts for refusal and 50 harmless prompts for over-refusal, with 13 continuation phrases scored per prompt. Under amendment A2.1, MMLU-Pro and WikiText log-perplexity are the candidate endpoints. HellaSwag, KL and the refusal stages are descriptive. % D8-BEGIN
The TF-v1 artefacts were regenerated on 6 November 2026 under source hash \texttt{a224779bf873} (run \texttt{20261106-062239-29d865d2}): the KL cache content root, the G1 repeats, the G2 bridge rows and the recoverable requalification rows all reproduce the retained summaries (Appendix~\ref{sec:repro}). The round-1 and round-2 pilot run ids below are the original, lost runs.
% D8-END

Timing was the first gate. The round-2 timing pilot (run \texttt{20261105-144427-fb58ffe6}) measured a warm median of 1.805 min per entry against a 1.40 min gate. Amendment A2.4 batches the HellaSwag and refusal stages and keeps MMLU-Pro, KL and WikiText at batch 1. On 229 stratum entries scored under one source hash, repeat scoring on 10 entries was bitwise identical (maximum discrepancy 0). The signal-reliability lower 95\% bound was 0.990 for MMLU-Pro gold-letter log-probability and 0.992 for WikiText log-perplexity. WikiText log-perplexity met every pre-registered qualification condition, with a maximum item half-width of 0.013 against $\delta = 0.049$. MMLU-Pro gold log-probability did not. Its maximum item half-width was 0.114, against a limit of 0.10. A family-weighted 17.6\% of entries exceeded $\delta/2$, with a 97.5\% upper bound of 22.6\% against a 20\% limit. Unweighted, 42 of 229 entries (18.3\%) exceed it. The exceedances are concentrated in large-effect edits, because item half-width tracks the absolute effect (Spearman 0.92). The pre-registered confirmatory test of H1a was therefore not run, and H1a is untested.

The pre-specified gate that would have upgraded H1a to confirmatory was not met. The gate used 4,000 simulated panels per scenario and 4,000 bootstrap draws per interval, with seed 20261105, and it chose no interval procedure on the simulated data. In simulations calibrated on the development out-of-fold losses, the lower 95\% bound on the power at $\theta = 0.15$ was 0.43 to 0.47 against the 0.80 required. No interval procedure met both the null-size limit (0.035) and the coverage limit (0.93) in every scenario. H1a is therefore exploratory and power-limited. Estimates and intervals are reported with no pass or kill label. The sealed fresh panel was not scored.

On the same development panel, a fixed StatNN predictor on weight statistics reduced out-of-fold residual MAE relative to an isotonic function of incremental norm by 12.9\% (effective sample size 39.7, about 40 component equivalents). The 95\% intervals from three procedures, each conditional on items and joint over items, run from $-$0.8\% to 24.3\%. Five of the six lower bounds are negative. The sixth, from the conditional percentile interval, is +0.3 points, so the data do not distinguish a modest residual readability from none. The estimate is exploratory. It is carried by large-effect edits: LoRA SFT 23\%, chain 9\% and task vector $-$5\%. With the 42 entries whose item half-width exceeds $\delta/2$ left out, it falls to 0.7\% [$-$20\%, 15\%]. That leave-out drops rows from the scored sums only. The nuisance terms, StatNN and the isotonic comparator were still fit on all 229 stratum rows and the 157 null-family rows. The estimate replicates across disjoint item halves (10.5\% and 11.7\%) and has not been tested on fresh components. % D8-BEGIN

The companion analyses that the adjudication made mandatory are in Table~\ref{tab:h1a-companions}. They are descriptive and post hoc, and they use the development panel only. They add no registered claim and change no status. Unless a row says otherwise, each uses the 229 stratum rows, and each interval is a component-bootstrap percentile interval over 4,000 draws. The cells were chosen after the fact, so no interval in the table is confirmatory.

With a flexible nuisance (a family-by-natural-spline term in log incremental norm, fit on training rows only), $\hat\theta$ is 0.135, and a shared spline with family slopes gives 0.126. Applying the all-six rule, under which an estimate is distinguishable from zero only if all six lower bounds are positive, the flexible estimate is not distinguishable from zero. Its six lower bounds lie between $-$0.005 and 0.008.

The other outcome-free readings are positive: equal component weights give 0.176, the high-norm tertile gives 0.195, and LoRA SFT alone gives 0.230. Several percentile intervals in the table exclude zero, but none is confirmatory. The signal is absent in task vectors ($-$0.048, 30 rows, 27 components). It is negative in the lowest-norm tertile of chain steps ($-$0.776, 42 rows, 27 components), which is one of 27 stratum cells in the companion file.

The estimate does not survive inverse-square weighting by the item half-width $h_i$, which gives $-$0.257 [$-$0.666, 0.074]. The 42 rows with $h_i > \delta/2$ carry 0.09\% of that weight. Restricting the sum to the 187 rows with $h_i \le \delta/2$, under the fit on all rows, gives 0.007 [$-$0.164, 0.154]. Refitting the nuisance and StatNN without the 42 wide rows gives 0.119 [0.0003, 0.231] on 187 rows. The two leave-out readings disagree, so the 0.7\% reported above depends on the fitting convention. The leave-out results do not show that the signal is item noise either.

The 20 random item splits all give positive estimates. Their means, across the four combinations of split halves, run from 0.109 to 0.126.
